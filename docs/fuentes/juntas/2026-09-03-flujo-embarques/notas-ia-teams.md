Current Sampling Process: The current process weighs and samples concentrate shipments truck by truck. The process applies to concentrates such as lead and zinc and involves information from the mine, laboratory, and receiving or destination units.

Excel-Based Reporting: Shipment, sample, laboratory, and assay information is currently transferred and reviewed through Excel files. SAFSA-TRN-Sj-SanJulian described this manual flow as difficult to control across the different stages and susceptible to reporting or transcription errors.

Desired Automation: The requested solution should capture information from the existing preferred tool, route it through a review workflow, and provide visibility into changes and approvals. The system should reduce manual entry and allow users to follow the status of samples, lots, results, and reports from one place.

Lot-Level Reporting: Closed-lot reports group shipments by lot and display dates, customer, unit, product, wet and dry weights, differences, and assay elements including gold, silver, lead, copper, zinc, arsenic, iron, cadmium, antimony, mercury, and related measures.
.
Weight Calculation: The total lot weight is calculated as the sum of the weights of the trucks that make up the lot, including the applicable mine and receiving-unit records. SAFSA-TRN-Sj-SanJulian identified this total as the basis for weighted element calculations.

Weighted Results: The system should calculate weighted assay values for the lot rather than treating every truck result as an independent final result. The discussion distinguished weighted values from average values and indicated that the calculation should be available by element and by lot.

Result Sources: The process compares or reports results from the mine, the customer or receiving unit, and third-party analysis where applicable. The report must preserve the source and context of each result so users can determine which value is used for settlement or comparison.

Unit Conversion: The discussion identified inconsistent reporting units, including kilograms per tonne, grams per tonne, and related assay representations. The system must standardize or clearly convert units so that values such as silver and other assays are reported consistently in the required format.

Automatic Identifiers: The proposed workflow should automatically carry key identifiers such as the lot number, mine or unit, customer, product, and sampling or entry information. This would reduce repeated manual capture and support retrieval of the complete record.

Date Traceability: SAFSA-TRN-Sj-SanJulian requested that sample, result-entry, and update dates be captured automatically where possible. If a result is corrected, the system should retain the original context and add or preserve an update date rather than obscuring the history.

Controlled Corrections: The workflow should show who entered or authorized reported values and which steps are defined or variable. Corrections and modifications need to remain visible for review, with an audit trail covering the change and its authorization.

Reanalysis Handling: A reanalysis may be requested when a third-party result or another assay falls outside the expected range. The system should record the initial result, the reanalysis, and subsequent analysis-in-conjunto or comparable results as separate events.

Example Review: The demonstrated reconciliation example showed multiple gold and silver values from different sources, including a first third-party result, a reanalysis, a joint analysis, and a later analysis. Out-Of-Range Results: The process includes identifying results outside an agreed range and determining the next action, such as an additional analysis or review. The system should make these exceptions visible rather than allowing them to remain only in a manually maintained spreadsheet.
